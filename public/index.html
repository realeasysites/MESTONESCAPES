Two hands and a pile of stones.

That’s where Dan’s passion has always been. A dry stone wall holds together without any mortar, only the weight of each stone and how well it’s placed.

Built right, it drains freely, rides out a Maine winter and stands for generations. Built wrong, it’s on the ground in a few seasons. New England’s old walls show plenty of both.

We use local stone wherever we can, often stone from the property itself, along with reclaimed granite. And we build to the methods of the Dry Stone Walling Association of Great Britain, learned from master wallers in England and Scotland.

  • CraftsmanshipWe keep learning so every project is our best work.
  • IntegrityWe’re committed to the project and the craft, and to giving clients what they need for it to succeed.
  • ValueStonework that lasts, from a crew that knows the stone sources and building traditions of Maine and New England.

What we build

From a single set of granite steps to hundreds of feet of farm wall.

  • Dry stone walls

    Freestanding farmers walls, retaining walls, sitting walls and lace walls, built from stone on your site or sourced locally.

  • Wall restoration

    Old New England walls taken down and restacked the way they were first built, so they stand for another hundred years.

  • Granite steps & walkways

    Reclaimed granite steps, curbing walkways, herringbone brick and granite, even steps carved into the ledge.

  • Terraces & patios

    Dry-laid terraces in local boulders and glacial till stone, pool patios and granite flagging.

  • Pillars, fireplaces & features

    Granite entry pillars, beach stone fireplaces, a public memorial labyrinth: stone as the centerpiece.

  • Sculpture & arches

    Gothic arches with reclaimed keystones and balanced stone sculpture, the work that started it all on the beach at Fort Williams.

How a project comes together

  1. Tell us about it

    Call, text or send the form below. Photos of the site help if you have them.

  2. Walk the site

    We look at the ground, the drainage, any existing stone and how the work should sit in the landscape.

  3. Choose the stone

    Stone from your property, local fieldstone or reclaimed granite. We know where to find it.

  4. Build it by hand

    Every stone placed by hand, to DSWA methods, by the crew that sees it through.

Where we build
